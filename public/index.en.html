01
STRAIGHTFORWARD HIKE

What to pack
for an easier day.

A valley, forest loop or low summit does not need expedition styling. It needs a compact system that survives rain, a blister and a two-hour delay.

WEAR / LAYERS

Three layers that work together

  • Breathable T-shirt or light base layer.
  • Light fleece for pauses.
  • Windproof and waterproof shell within easy reach.
  • Broken-in trail shoes or boots with dependable grip.
  • Hiking socks and a cap or sun hat.
PACK / CORE

The small things that rescue a day

  • Water split between two containers, food and a reserve snack.
  • Phone, offline map, power bank and short cable.
  • Small first-aid kit, blister care and personal medication.
  • Headlamp, even when the plan ends in daylight.
  • SPF, glasses and a bag for your own rubbish.
LITTLE HACKS / 04
Split the water.

Two bottles are safer than one and balance the pack better.

Keep dry items sealed.

Phone, documents and a spare layer belong in a dry bag.

Keep the shell on top.

If it requires unpacking everything, it will go on too late.

Test footwear first.

A long mountain day is the wrong debut for new shoes or socks.

DON’T

Do not rely on cotton as the only warm layer, an unknown stream, a hut that “must be open” or 18% phone battery. Stay on the marked trail, including for photos.

02
HIGH-MOUNTAIN HIKE

What to pack
when terrain gets serious.

Exposure, large ascent, rock and artificial aids raise the consequence of mistakes. Equipment matters, but experience, technique and a Plan B matter more.

WEAR / SYSTEM

Warmth without overheating

  • Base layer, fleece, insulating layer and waterproof shell.
  • Warm hat or buff; thin durable gloves for chains.
  • Stable familiar footwear with reliable grip.
  • Glasses and SPF, even in cloud.
  • A helmet where terrain and rockfall risk justify it.
PACK / MARGIN

Everything above, plus reserve

  • More water and food than perfect timing requires.
  • Dry warm layer and emergency blanket.
  • Offline or paper map, full power bank and headlamp.
  • First-aid kit, whistle, TOPR number and Ratunek app.
  • Technical equipment only when matched by skill and current conditions.
DECISION KIT / 04
Start early.

Still respect current TPN access rules and finish before darkness.

Watch the group.

The most tired person sets the pace and continuation decision.

Thunder means down.

Ridges, chains and metal aids are not storm shelters.

Winter is a separate skill.

Crampons, an axe and avalanche equipment help only when you know how to use them.

TURN AROUND / NO DEBATE

Turn around for thunderstorms, strong wind, wet or icy rock, lost markings, broken timing or fear that disrupts movement technique. Microspikes are not crampons and do not make steep winter terrain safe.

FINAL CHECK / MORNING OFPack the system.
Let conditions decide.

Before every start, recheck the route, forecast, TPN tourist bulletin and — with snow cover — the TOPR avalanche bulletin. Pack volumes are day-hike references, not safety guarantees.

BASE CAMP / WHERE TO STAY

Choose the morning,
not just the room.

Without a car and without fixed routes, the area around the railway and bus station is the most flexible. Once the priority trailheads are clear, sleeping closer to them can save a large amount of morning and evening energy.
